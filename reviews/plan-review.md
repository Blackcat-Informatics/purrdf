## Design findings

No required findings are open.

The plan addresses both the ordinary SIPS path and hybrid groups, and defines
connectivity through variables rather than matching constants. First-new-factor
decomposition is a concrete semi-naive eligibility rule; negative outer-variable
coupling prevents unsafe projection. Necessary multi-factor head output is
distinguished from avoidable body intermediates.

The proof frontier design explicitly handles the two important saturation
boundaries. A globally saturated proof height permits raw source heights through
`u32::MAX`, rather than incorrectly excluding witnesses at that boundary. If the
minimum attainable global summed height saturates, sum ceases to distinguish
winners and the pure lexical frontier is necessary. Otherwise the sum-first
frontier applies under the chosen height threshold. Exhaustive witness equality,
height masking and saturation cases are assigned, not assumed already passed.

Opaque guards remain stage-major multiplicity/order barriers. The plan correctly
does not infer replayability from `GuardReads::Bindings`: a fresh-blank or assign
implementation can consume state. Its tests cover errors reached after an earlier
successful candidate and therefore do not authorize arbitrary first-witness
short-circuiting of callbacks. Canonical confirmations share the preference law.

Round-wide admission is feasible without per-rule budget multiplication. The
planned contract requires one refusal sentinel, total refusal before closure
commit, and identical successful/refused counters across workers. Implementing
the credit pool must latch that sentinel: a spent check that merely breaks and
returns a successful prefix would violate Task 4. The plan requires the decisive
tests and does not claim that consumer checks bound callback-owned vectors.

Planner and calculus version changes are correctly distinguished from the
unchanged authored clause hash. Changed budget observations and assumed witness
preference do not qualify for an identical-observable-results exemption.
