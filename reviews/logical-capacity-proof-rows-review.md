The other listed consumers enumerate concrete frozen datasets or the builder being validated. Their indices are below the admitted count and the reserved `u32::MAX` niche index. None of these reasons asserts a bound on an arbitrary trait view.
- **Global resident terms (rows 20–21, 28).** `GlobalDictionary` inserts into its private hash only after the `GlobalTerms` check and `checked_from_index`. `value_index` and stack snapshot enumerate its resident nonzero-sized `Vec<GlobalInternedTerm>`, whose addressable length on supported 32/64-bit targets cannot reach the reserved `u64::MAX` index. These are resident vector indices, not arbitrary global logical IDs.
- **Segmented mapping (rows 33–36).** `with_first_term_index` checks `first + limits.terms`; `intern_batch` admits the actual term budget before returning IDs. `global` receives the ID just minted by that builder, and `local` checks subtraction plus the stored builder count. The private `map_id` calls are confined to `encode_dataset`; `seal` supplies a bounded builder, and `from_pack_v1` checks its source count against the supplied limits first. For every valid local index, `first + index < first + terms <= u64::MAX`. `encode_dataset` itself enumerates concrete frozen terms. The class must stay tied to these callers; `map_id(id, first)` in isolation is not safe.
- **Permutation and encoded shape (rows 7, 10–12).** The native frozen quad bound equals the resident permutation bound, so `try_permutation` cannot return a logical capacity refusal through `permutation`; physical allocation can still fail. `FileLayout::plan` validates its private section count before `header` narrows it. The two matrix assertions compare encoded byte lengths derived from checked dimensions against private fixed-width scalar writers; they are value-shape assertions, not logical-capacity admission. No input-dependent count is blessed by these three shape rows.
