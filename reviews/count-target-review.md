## C4: aggregate instruction floor does not prove selected-worker coverage

**Resolved for the replacement artifact; original finding accepted by the writer/root.** The earlier `worker_sample` enabled before `pool.broadcast`. Non-target callbacks returned immediately, allowing their Rayon schedulers to execute while the selected worker performed its fixed loop. The collector required only a process-total count of at least one million instructions. Exact event count/enabled/running equality establishes full coverage of the configured event, but does not identify which thread supplied it. Counts increased from **6,005,090** at one worker to **8,705,394–9,840,027** at 32; that excess alone exceeded the one-million threshold. Consequently the original floor could succeed even if the selected worker were omitted. Those completed 37-index records/signatures remain valid observations, but their prior all-worker qualification is withdrawn rather than transferred to later artifacts.

The adopted repair starts the broadcast while counters are disabled, waits until every non-target callback is parked, and makes the selected existing worker own acknowledged Enable, the complete fixed loop and acknowledged Disable. Other callbacks are released only afterward. Source inspection of rayon-core **1.13.0** confirms that the ordinary external caller blocks: `broadcast/mod.rs:97–120` passes `WorkerThread::current() == None` to `CountLatch::with_count`, `latch.rs:365–399` selects Blocking, and LockLatch waits on a Condvar. It does not steal scheduler work. The fixture keeps that caller outside Rayon, uses bounded actionable rendezvous/release waits, and retains the full value/index/non-caller assertions. No per-thread counter framework or production seam is needed.

**Adopted-source and native recheck: PASS for the bound replacement artifact.** The new `worker_sample` asserts that its external caller is outside Rayon, starts the broadcast disabled and makes every callback rendezvous through the same Mutex/Condvar. Non-target callbacks cannot return to the scheduler until released; the target waits for all arrivals, drops the guard, then owns required control, acknowledged Enable, the full loop and acknowledged Disable. ### Broader allocation opportunities after full recovery

These are separate follow-up candidates, with source evidence and no measured saving claimed:

- **Reuse owned output storage in the common concatenation home.** `parallel::concatenate_chunks` currently allocates a new outer Vec while every successful chunk already owns one. Reusing an appropriate owned typed chunk buffer after source-error selection could avoid one allocation/copy; check actual capacity, retained excess and typed reservation rather than assuming reuse always wins. Group input/output cardinalities differ, so BIND's one-to-one buffer proof cannot be copied wholesale to grouping.
- **Move worker mints without the intermediate tail copy.** `ScratchInterner::take_values_from` uses `split_off` and then constructs `(hash, value)` records. A zero-local-prefix worker can potentially move its entire values Vec instead of allocating the split tail. The hash/record conversion and nonzero/shared-prefix cases still need one coherent home, mint order and dangling-ID proof.
- **Avoid storing fresh terms twice during minting output.** `parallel::portable_row` clones each fresh TermValue into an owned row Vec, while governed `WorkerLedger::values` also owns those same mints for ghost replay. A shared owned mint representation or row references into the harvest could reduce copies. Preserve source-ordered IDs, ghost deduplication, precise refusal prefixes, portability of returned rows and fallible dataset reads; this is broader ownership work, not a current shortcut.
- **Bound cache lifetime or borrow parsed numeric values.** Workers begin with an empty XSD parse cache; `expr::xsd_of_term` stores `parsed.clone()` and returns another clone, and cache hits also clone. Shared immutable values or carefully shorter cache lifetime can reduce large-value copies/peak. Benchmark repeated and unique literals, and preserve fallible-view read/error behavior. Retiring the cache without evidence can lose the very reuse that saves instructions.
- **Use the existing SmallVec home for genuinely small group lists.** `modifier::eval_group` owns one heap Vec of row ordinals per group; the measured fixture has four rows in each of 5,000 groups. An inline ordinal list could remove small-list allocations, with spill for large groups. Account for larger map entries and preserve first-seen group order/empty-group behavior. The already planned canonical integer/inline/admitted-scratch migration remains recovery work and is not deferred into this follow-up list.
