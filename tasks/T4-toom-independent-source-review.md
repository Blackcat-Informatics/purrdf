# Independent Toom-3 source review

Reviewed source: numeric branch HEAD `2a1a15dcef2b97d87f0af29a208253db0b7df596` plus uncommitted `crates/xsd/src/bigint/compat.rs` and `crates/xsd/src/exact/cost.rs` diff, SHA-256 `5fe129d61aaed43f1383bf51b0cbf768592dc56e14c81be42bc2c41b8c1a851b`. `git diff --check` passed. This is a read-only source review; I did not independently rerun native/WASM tests or instruction probes.

**Current verdict: PASS for the material source behavior and 512-limb cost premise reviewed here; no issue or performance qualification.** Dispatch again selects balanced Toom only with both operands at least 512 limbs and the shorter greater than twice `ceil(longer/3)`. This restores the stated `n >= 512`, `s >= 171` premise in `exact::cost::mul` and the physical eight-result argument. At the smallest balanced case `n=m=512,s=171`, the claimed physical bound is `2n+28s+64=5876 < 8(n+m)=8192` words; at the most asymmetric eligible cutoff `n=765,m=512,s=255`, it is `8734 < 10216`. The five-child work envelope plus the stated local `128s+128` allowance fits the parent `n*m+16(n+m)+1` at these tight shapes, with increasing quadratic slack above the cutoff. Point-product owners are scoped away before one-destination recombination. These are source-bound checks of the stated conservative law, not instrumented proof for every recursive shape.

`eval_positive_point` forms `a0 + point*a1 + point^2*a2` for points 1 and 2 in a single admitted destination. A maximum input limb plus two words covers the factor-four carry; the `u128` intermediate safely holds one word times a factor at most four plus a word and carry. Shared `add_scaled_magnitude_at` checks offset plus source length and propagated carry against the destination, returning typed `SizeOverflow` if either cannot fit. Its factor-one calls preserve the old chunked Karatsuba accumulation and place each nonnegative Toom coefficient at its polynomial limb offset in a checked `la+lb` destination. The outer sign is applied only after canonical trimming. Refusal drops the owned partially written destination; it is not returned. Direct Decimal multiplication reaches this dispatch through `Integer::mul_using` and `BigInt::mul_fast_using`.

The new exact `/2` interpolation branch checks the low magnitude bit in all builds before shifting. `BigInt::shr_using(1, storage)` shifts magnitude and reapplies sign, so for an even negative numerator it agrees with signed exact division and uses the same fallible admitted storage. The three half-step numerators are even by the interpolation identities; the `/3` step still uses quotient/remainder and checks its remainder. A failed parity assertion would expose a broken internal interpolation invariant rather than silently round a coefficient.

The prior review blocked the **experimental 256-limb** version because its cost argument still assumed `n >= 512`; its loose five-child maximum exceeded the parent charge at `n=381,m=256`. That finding is historical and resolved for current source by restoring the 512-limb cutoff. It was a proof gap, not a demonstrated numerical undercharge. The owner reports current-source behavior differential 1/1, governed peak 1/1, native independent replay 4/4 and actual Node/WASM replay 4/4; I did not rerun them. The 512-limb threshold still lacks a qualifying performance result, and the remaining large-transform, division, radix, architecture and host work required for #443 is unfinished.
