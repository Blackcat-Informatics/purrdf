This is a source-bound review of prepared DateTime/Time/Duration rendering and admitted calendar/duration casts. Temporal arithmetic, ADJUST, SECONDS and exact-work charging are separately OPEN in `T1-temporal-root-resource-review.md` and are excluded here.

**Verdict: scoped source PASS for renderers and casts reviewed; no material defect identified in these paths.** `prepare_calendar_text` and `prepare_duration_text` call the exact seconds `render_layout` and pass its native temporary bound to the caller before `prepare_text` performs decimal-group conversion. The shared layout covers both quotient destinations and group storage; prepared text retains the actual group allocation. Duration rendering scans those already prepared groups for day remainder and fraction, writes the day quotient directly to the final formatter, and creates no exact quotient or fractional coefficient owner. A scalar remainder stays below 86,400, so each long-division output digit is at most 9. The sign is taken from the duration's sign-coherent components; zero emits the correct subtype zero. DateTime/Time render the prepared seconds with a two-digit integral field and preserve timezone, year and original fractional scale. Time's 24:00:00 lexical is normalized to the same midnight value as 00:00:00 at `read_time`; DateTime retains its separate end-of-day handling.

The evaluator's `NumericFrame` keeps the original temporary grant in `CalendarText`/`DurationText` through `workspace.literal`, and the borrowed parsed value remains live during formatting. `finish_prepared` refuses if the retained prepared capacity exceeds the admitted amount. The output literal gets its own workspace admission while the prepared temporary grant is still live. For casts, `copy_exact_seconds_admitted` asks for the copy's heap bytes before `try_copy`, and the evaluator transfers that grant to `ParsedValue` before releasing the source. Calendar identity, DateTimeStamp and DateTime-to-Time copies use this path; Date/Gregorian forms have no seconds heap. Duration subtype casts copy only the kept seconds component: a mixed duration can become either subtype, and a cross-subtype cast yields the target zero. Discarded seconds need no allocation. Storage refusal remains typed and is forwarded by the evaluator rather than being collapsed into a value-space refusal.
