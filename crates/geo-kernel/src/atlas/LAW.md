# Original curves in the geodetic-normal atlas

A native curve retains its mathematical law, original carrier ordinates and
reference binding. Coordinate-linear edges follow the complete signed written
longitude change. A unique endpoint geodesic retains both original endpoints and
the private shortest-branch certificate. An azimuth/length edge retains its
original start, exact azimuth and exact metre length. A transformed edge retains
its original source coordinates, operation chain, epoch and actual geographic
target. Quantized direct and transformation outputs are materialization evidence,
never endpoints of these continuous mathematical curves.

The unit geodetic normal provides a smooth atlas through longitude cuts and
coordinate poles. For an arc, its normal and derivative come from the same
auxiliary-sphere line equations used by direct propagation. Coordinate-linear
normal images derive from the exact affine source angles. Continuous operation
images use the complete original parameter interval and whole-panel derivative.
The shared normal equation and its Taylor specialization have one arithmetic
home. Chart longitude derivatives can be absent at a pole; the smooth normal
and its derivative remain valid there.

A pair contact solver excludes complete normal images before choosing a two
component chart. If both images have the same strict sign in the omitted
component, equality of the other components and unit length proves equality of
the omitted component. The shared interval root solver proves existence and
uniqueness by whole-box contraction. Original endpoint identities can supply
exact root existence only after that contraction has been proved. An interval
containing zero, an approximate local iterate, and an output-coordinate epsilon
are never equality witnesses. Geometric subdivision and increased arithmetic
precision preserve the original curve law. Unresolved decisions refuse.

Exact same-start, same normalized-azimuth branches share the shorter complete
physical path. The shared interval is [0,min(lengths)/length] in each original
parameterization. This exact branch-overlap evidence keeps its endpoints symbolic
and does not fabricate geographic coordinates from a rounded direct result.

## Oriented native rings

An immutable OrientedRing certifies exact closure and a Jordan boundary before
selecting its Left side. A positive unique shortest arc cannot self-intersect,
since removing its loop would produce a shorter path. Other complete edge images
are partitioned into proved monotone normal charts and checked for all contacts.
Only the declared adjacent endpoints and the closing junction may meet. A direct
endpoint quantized to fifteen places never establishes exact ring closure.

For a query unit normal q and boundary normal n(t), use the one-form

    A = (−q × n) · n' / (1 − q · n).

For a proper oriented Jordan region, its complete integral is the area of the
Left side on the unit normal sphere minus 4π when q belongs to that side. The
proper Left area lies strictly between zero and 4π. A strictly negative complete
integral therefore proves Interior, and a strictly positive complete integral
proves Exterior. Exact source and symmetry contacts prove Boundary first. This
law distinguishes a larger complementary side without choosing a smaller area.

Source-linear and geodesic density panels use the shared symmetric Taylor
integrator. Its complete derivative enclosure, normalized by the derivative
factorial, proves the remainder; differences between successive quadratures do
not. Transformed panels use their complete fixed-interval density image. The
priority refinement replaces the largest admitted uncertain panel. Exact lower
and upper endpoint sums live in FixedIntervalSum: replacing a panel removes its
retained endpoints exactly before adding the child enclosures, without repeated
interval-subtraction widening. Output, work, depth, precision and simultaneous
storage limits admit the complete proof or return a typed refusal.

A native polygon explicitly selects Left or Right. Left is the intersection of
the supplied rings' Left sides; clockwise hole traversals therefore remove the
hole interior. Right is the closed complement of that complete intersection.
The separate Written/Complement selector acts on this immutable base. Native
curved polygons have no fabricated coordinate-linear chart or chart area.

A Jordan ring made entirely from coordinate-linear edges has an exact chart
shortcut when its original endpoints close without longitude aliases, none is
a pole, and the complete written longitude span is less than 360 degrees.
On that open strip the normal map is injective and orientation preserving:
the longitude/latitude area Jacobian has the strictly positive factor
`cos(latitude)`. The bounded planar Jordan interior therefore maps to the
physical Left side for a positive shoelace orientation, and to the Right side
for a negative orientation. The existing exact identified-atlas point predicate
and signed-area body supply that decision. Full-turn parallels, pole junctions,
nonlinear edges and unresolved orientation retain their original certified
normal-atlas proof. This shortcut neither chooses a smaller region nor changes
point membership.

## Original-parameter boundary arrangements

Complete contacts between every pair of distinct validated rings divide each
original edge into connected open fragments. Every isolated contact has a
certified ordered source-parameter enclosure. Overlap endpoints retain their
exact source witness or selected-branch parameter proof. Exact source endpoints
are original ring nodes. Intervals that cannot yet be ordered cause further
precision refinement; overlapping receipts alone never identify two roots.

A contact node is named by the exact original-region identity, the original
edge pair, its certified source-order occurrence and its overlap endpoint when
applicable. An original endpoint is named by its original ring address. Proven
coincident node classes select the smallest of these identities. The finite
union-find structure uses union by rank, so every metadata lookup has a checked
logarithmic depth bound. Numerical bounds, execution limits, compiler identities
and equivalent proofs are excluded from these node and fragment identities.

Between two ordered contact enclosures there is a strict rational parameter.
Complete pair-contact exclusion proves that no other boundary meets its open
fragment. Membership of every nonincident ring is constant on that fragment;
an exact original coordinate-linear image or a complete unrounded image box
classifies the representative through the existing ring predicate. This
representative labels a fragment after separation has been proved. It is never
a sampled substitute for complete separation.

The original ring's Left state is true on the fragment's local left side.
Every ring coincident with that fragment has the same state for equal traversal
and the opposite state for reversed traversal. Changing sides toggles all those
incident ring states simultaneously. The one polygon/intersection/union/
complement Boolean law selects a physical boundary exactly when the two region
states differ, and orients it with the selected interior on its left. A shared
fragment is emitted once by original edge order. Internal union walls vanish;
whole-union complementation reverses boundary orientation.

The resulting subcurve still borrows its original edge and has two source-defined
parameters. Refining a parameter reruns the same original contact law and keeps
the node identity. Irrational crossing coordinates and uncertain endpoint tails
remain implicit; no fifteen-place materialized coordinate replaces them.
Consumers integrate or bound those complete endpoint enclosures under their own
global error budget. Work, memory, output and cancellation refusal publishes no
partial arrangement.

## Exact point witnesses

The shared point/curve predicate identifies all source seam and pole aliases.
Unique shortest equatorial and meridian symmetries provide original-law contacts.
For opposite exact endpoint latitudes, the half-turn about a query equatorial
axis can interchange the exact endpoints. Uniqueness fixes the arclength midpoint
at the query or its antipode. A shortest positive arc has no repeated point, so a
fixed point can occur only at that midpoint. Complete midpoint normal-image
exclusion of the other fixed point proves exact contact, rather than treating a
small residual as zero.

Selected equatorial and meridian arc extents compare the original metre length
against certified original integrals. At a north pole the outgoing meridian is
180 degrees minus the original azimuth; at a south pole it is the azimuth.
These are the longitude-zero source frames. Traversal lengths can include full
revolutions and preserve their branch. An unresolved endpoint-length equality
requires more precision or refuses.

## Closed rectangle and footprint predicates

Native region covers and continuous-image membership share one complete
original-curve/closed-rectangle predicate. Region covers prepare the selected
physical union boundary once, retaining its exact noded/coalesced source
segments and complete selected native curves. Internal component walls and
identified chart cuts do not change the successful cover of the same physical
set. Arrangement work and retained storage remain in the invocation budget;
an unresolved arrangement never publishes a cover selected from internal walls.
Exact original endpoints, including
physical pole identification, or a certified curve point within the rectangle
prove contact. Every original parameter panel must otherwise be proved outside.
Possible contact triggers subdivision and arithmetic refinement. Unresolved
arithmetic and tangencies refuse; they do not select a successful coarser cover.

Longitudes compare all admitted whole-turn aliases. Across a meridian pole,
longitude separation alone cannot discard a panel. Strict containment of an
indexed pole phase (k+1/2)π in the complete monotone auxiliary-phase image proves
that the original curve crosses the corresponding physical pole. A footprint
rectangle containing that pole intersects it at every pole longitude spelling.

The source-linear predicate first checks exact endpoints and bounding boxes.
After box overlap, a vertical or horizontal closed segment necessarily meets
the rectangle and needs no rational crossing construction. The remaining four
segment intersections use their one exact predicate home. That original body
admits each arithmetic operation at its actual reduced operand widths before
products, divisions, reductions, comparisons and result cloning. Rectangle
comparisons admit the actual operand pair before each short-circuit decision;
source integer coordinates are never assigned a wider footprint denominator.
Pure legacy
predicates and admitted geographic calls execute the same body. Numerical phases
similarly use one admission and charging home, including failure exits and
callback environment revalidation.

## Exact axis-plane contacts

A certified unique shortest path is fixed pointwise by every ellipsoid isometry
that fixes its two endpoints. Equatorial reflection therefore confines a unique
path with equatorial endpoints to the equator. Reflection in a meridian plane
confines a unique meridian endpoint path to that plane. Two distinct meridian
planes meet the surface only at the exact poles; an equatorial and meridian
plane meet only at the two exact opposite equatorial positions. The complete
intersection classifier checks both candidates with the shared original point
law. It publishes an endpoint-only answer only when every common candidate is
already an exact original endpoint witness. Shared planes and any other
candidate retain the general certified contact/overlap path. This proof avoids
ill-conditioned numerical root isolation at an already proved pole endpoint.

Two unique shortest paths on the same exact equatorial or meridian plane use
one complete circle-phase overlap proof. Equatorial phase is original longitude.
Reflection in either plane is an ellipsoid isometry fixing both endpoints.
A unique shortest curve equals its reflection, so it lies in that fixed plane.
For a meridian frame, phase is original latitude on that longitude and
`180-latitude` on its opposite longitude; exact poles have phase ±90 independent
of their source longitude. The signed endpoint phase change is reduced below
180 degrees. Meridian ground scale is strictly positive and 180-degree periodic,
so the shorter phase interval is exactly the shorter physical meridian interval.
Uniqueness excludes a half-turn tie. Intersecting the complete intervals over
the three possible whole-turn aliases proves every overlap, including pole and
longitude-cut crossings, without a rank-deficient numerical contact solver.

Each overlap endpoint is an original endpoint of at least one input arc. The
published endpoint coordinates retain that exact source witness. Parameter
receipts enclose unrounded distance from the arc start divided by its unrounded
original complete length; every subarc of a unique shortest path is itself
shortest. Meridian arclength fractions are generally irrational and are never
replaced by angular ratios or rounded micrometre distances. These enclosures
are proof evidence, separate from mathematical curve inputs and identities.

The same original-witness law proves a general contained subarc when both of
its original endpoints have independently proved membership on the other
unique shortest arc. Every subarc of a shortest path is shortest: replacing
one by a shorter path would shorten the original complete path. The contained
input has a certified unique branch, so it equals that complete subcurve,
including reversed traversal. Exact physical endpoint equality selects a
constant contact; distinct endpoints require strictly ordered parameter
enclosures before publishing positive overlap. If ordinary inverse proof
bounds cannot separate the parameters, stricter unrounded source proofs are
requested from the same solver. Unknown endpoint membership retains the full
contact search and never proves disjointness.

Partial overlap has the same proof when an original endpoint of each input is
proved strictly inside the other input and the two witnesses are physically
distinct. Unique shortest subpaths between the witnesses coincide. Equality
on an open subpath supplies identical tangent data, and uniqueness of the
smooth geodesic initial-value equation continues their common supporting line.
The remaining original endpoints extend away from the witnessed interval,
so its endpoints are the complete intersection endpoints. More generally the
intersection of two unique shortest paths is connected: between any two common
points both subpaths are the same unique shortest path. This rules out an
unreported disconnected shared piece. Endpoint parameter enclosures must lie
strictly between zero and one; unresolved comparisons refine the original
proofs or refuse. Both traversal directions and both input orders retain the
same exact original overlap endpoints.

An exact constant shortest curve is detected by original physical endpoint
equality, including seam and pole aliases. Its contact with another curve uses
the shared point-on-curve law. A positive answer retains the constant curve's
complete parameter interval `[0,1]` and encloses the other curve's parameter
from unrounded arclength. It never publishes a fictitious isolated two-variable
root or uses a rounded zero distance as an existence witness.

Written coordinate-linear constants require identical original longitude and
latitude, or two endpoints on the same exact pole row. Physical endpoint
aliases alone do not suffice: a written `-180` to `180` edge at a non-pole makes
a complete longitude turn. Its constant-point contacts retain both seam
parameter events. Zero-length selected azimuth arcs are constant independently
of their unobserved direction.

Selected axial geodesics repeat after `2*pi*a` on the equator and after four
exact meridian quarter integrals on a meridian. The shared point-law phase
construction provides first nonnegative travel; subsequent visits are that
travel plus each nonnegative integer multiple of the strictly positive period.
Every admitted visit has its complete distance interval at or below the exact
selected length; the first interval strictly above that length excludes all
later visits. A straddling length decision refines or refuses the complete
operation. Event parameters divide unrounded travel by exact original length.
These distinct intervals cannot be replaced by one full-domain enclosure or a
nearest-distance ratio that omits revolutions. Output, work and memory limits
refuse before partial publication.

For a general selected geodesic, principal radii `M,N` are at least
`m=min(a,b)^2/max(a,b)`, so its unit-speed ambient tangent turns at rate at most
`1/m`. If a repeated-point subarc had length `L<pi*m`, each tangent would have
angular distance less than `pi/2` from its midpoint tangent. Integrating their
strictly positive projections would give a nonzero endpoint chord, a
contradiction. A strict certified original-length bound below `pi*m` therefore
proves an exact original start contact has the sole nonconstant parameter zero.

Native side selection has one checked Boolean law for non-boundary points:
ring Left classes form the native intersection; written exterior and hole
classes form the carrier base. Oriented Right and the explicit polygon selector
act on the whole base, followed by union and any outer complement. Boundary
points retain the physical sector arrangement rather than being booleanized.
A validated native ring's uniform enclosure predicate borrows the same exact
curve and complete boundary classifier without constructing synthetic polygons.

Written-chart sectors use exact formal displacements `p+epsilon*d`, with
`epsilon` positive and sufficiently small, rather than materializing probe
coordinates. For a vertex height, the sign of
`vertex_y-p_y-epsilon*d_y` is its constant sign, followed by `-d_y` when the
constant is zero. For an edge `e=b-a`, the horizontal crossing abscissa minus
the query abscissa has the sign of
`(e cross (p-a)+epsilon*(e cross d))/e_y`. Its constant sign decides first;
the linear coefficient decides only at exact incidence. These are the same
half-open crossing predicates as the original point classifier. Closed-box
incidence uses the corresponding lexicographic endpoint comparisons. All
coefficients are exact rationals, and each product and reduction is admitted
before execution through the shared arithmetic cost home.

There are finitely many source edges. Therefore one positive displacement
interval preserves every nonzero constant sign simultaneously. Sector
directions are strict positive combinations of adjacent incident rays, so a
sector cannot lie on an incident edge. The formal signs give the same local
membership as every sufficiently small probe in that sector, without computing
the nearest remote intersection or selecting an epsilon. At the longitude cut,
the sign of the longitude direction selects the exact neighbouring chart:
`180+epsilon*d_x` with positive `d_x` is represented as
`-180+epsilon*d_x`, and conversely at `-180`. Pole neighbourhoods retain their
separate exact annulus/cut proof. This arithmetic improvement changes neither
physical membership nor the topology law.

A complete open written fragment already noded at every contact has only its
two local transverse sides. Their exact directions are `(-dy,dx)` and
`(dy,-dx)`, evaluated by the same formal union-direction predicate. No
non-collinear boundary can pass through the fragment interior after complete
noding. The physical union boundary contains the fragment exactly when those
two non-boundary side classes differ; unresolved side classes refuse. Whole
union complements exchange the classes and preserve this boundary decision.
Longitude-cut duplicates and internal union edges therefore disappear by the
same predicate, and a written pole row contributes no one-dimensional edge.
The proven transverse mode omits the ordinary ring-incidence scan while keeping
the same half-open winding body. Every unrestricted point or direction request
retains that scan; no incidence decision is weakened by the optimized mode.

The complete sector constructor has the same transverse proof: it collects
every incident edge ray before choosing an open sector. Validated written chart
vertices have longitude in `[-180,180]`. An edge translated by one full turn can
therefore meet a validated non-pole query only when the query is exactly on the
cut. Translating that query to its other cut spelling preserves incidence and
the endpoint-minus-query vectors. One borrowed query and at most one exact cut
alias replace the three translated copies of each edge; written-boundary
membership is recorded by the same complete incidence traversal.

After the exact half-open height test proves an edge straddles a horizontal ray,
its crossing longitude is a convex combination of the original endpoint
longitudes. A query strictly below both longitudes crosses, and one strictly
above both does not. At equality, a negative formal longitude direction proves
crossing when the query is at or below both endpoints; a nonnegative direction
proves no crossing when it is at or above both. This proof uses sufficiently
small positive displacements, including the exact constant-query case, and
does not select a numerical epsilon. Other endpoint cases retain the original
exact determinant or crossing quotient. Both the unrestricted and transverse
winding modes share this closed-interval proof and arithmetic admission.

For a validated source-linear Jordan ring whose complete written longitude
span is less than one full turn, exact coordinate-chart winding also decides
the oriented native side. A junction between two writings of the same exact
pole inserts a chart connector at that pole latitude; its complete physical
image is the single pole, so the nonpolar physical boundary is unchanged.
The normal map preserves orientation in the open chart, and the exact planar
orientation selects Left, including a region greater than a hemisphere. Every
longitude writing of a visited pole is Boundary. A pole not visited is outside
the bounded chart component before the explicit orientation complement. The
identified ±180-degree query uses the one alternative chart representative
when it lies in the written span. Nonpolar seam junctions and complete-turn
rings retain the general atlas proof; no full-turn connector is substituted
for an identified nonpolar point.
